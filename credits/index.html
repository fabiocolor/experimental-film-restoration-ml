Credits & Attribution

This independent research, its workflows, and the writing on this website are by Fabio P. Bedoya Huerta, except where another source is named.

The film frames and clips belong to their rights holders. They are shown here for research, criticism, documentation and education. A credit says where something came from; it is not permission to reuse it, and nothing here relicenses anyone else’s footage, models or software.

Research and source acknowledgements

  • Film names are given beside each example on the research pages and the talk pages.
  • The shots on the Black-and-White Colourization page are from US public-domain films: Prelinger Archives prints scanned by Internet Archive, and The General (1926) from an archive.org copy. Each record names its item.
  • Ross Lipman supplied the Frontier Experience telecine used as the historical reference on the Temporal CbCr page.
  • The flute players shot on the Diffusion Healing page is from the Wong Rengifo collection.
  • The faded trailers shown on the home page, the research pages and the talk pages (Reptilicus, Obsession, Unman, Wittering and Zigo, Point Blank, Juggernaut, Ben, Martin, Tales of the Bizarre) come from a 35mm trailer reel scan posted publicly on archive.org. The trailers belong to their rights holders.

For a peer-reviewed account of the earlier CopyCat research:

Fabio P. Bedoya Huerta, “Exploring Experimental Machine Learning in Film Restoration,” Archiving Conference, 22(1), 2025.

Models and machine-learning projects

  • Qwen-Image-Edit-2511, by the Qwen team.
  • Qwen-Image-Edit-2511-Lightning, by the LightX2V team.
  • MiniMax H3, by MiniMax.
  • MiniMax-H3-Fun-Controlnet-Union, by Alibaba PAI.
  • TeleStyle V2, by TeleAI.
  • Wan2.1, by the Wan team.
  • VACE, by its project contributors.

What a model puts out is an interpretation unless historical evidence backs it up. The original film element, and what is known about it, is what counts.

Software and technical projects

  • ComfyUI runs the downloadable workflows.
  • Nuke and CopyCat are where the CopyCat workflow runs.
  • DaVinci Resolve for conforming, colour management and review.
  • Phoenix, Loki, and DVO tools for restoration and preparation.
  • FFmpeg and HandBrake for preparing and encoding media.
  • Jekyll, Just the Docs, and GitHub Pages publish this website.

Product names and trademarks belong to their owners. They are named because they are what the research used, not as an endorsement.
