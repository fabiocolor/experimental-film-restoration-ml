# Knights of the Trail, the cabin: record

The nitrate example on the [diffusion healing]({{ '/diffusion-healing/' | relative_url }}) page and the home page. Shown by courtesy of the George Eastman Museum; the material is not offered for download.

<dl class="facts">
  <dt>Source</dt><dd>A nitrate element of <em>Knights of the Trail</em> (George Eastman Museum, element L202100080002), frames 734–959 of the shot: a cowboy at a table in a cabin. 226 frames, 24 fps, worked on a 640 × 512 working copy with a fixed crop. In 18 frames (748–749, 826–827, 871 and 937–949) the decay has consumed part of the picture; frames 749 and 827 come from a diacetate copy of the same shot.</dd>
  <dt>Route</dt><dd>Everything that moves (the actor, his hands, the bag) is kept from the film in every frame; the actor is found by comparing a clean frame with the still background. Only the damaged static background (the coat, the door, the wall, the table front and the areas marked in review) is generated, by Wan VACE 14B with masks, and joined to the original with a 24-pixel soft blend. The 206 undamaged frames are the scan, untouched.</dd>
  <dt>Models</dt><dd>Wan 2.1 VACE 14B, seed 0, 20 steps, CFG 3.5, PNG output.</dd>
  <dt>Record of what was generated</dt><dd>In the 18 damaged frames, 65 per cent of the pixels are original (the fifth version kept 77 per cent but left damage in place; the fourth kept 48). Frame 826, the worst, keeps 36 per cent; frame 937 keeps 89. A tinted version of the shot, with every generated pixel in red, is kept with the review and is the third still on the healing page.</dd>
  <dt>Review</dt><dd>Six versions reviewed in September and October 2026. The fifth was too conservative and left damage in the still background; the sixth, shown here, corrects that and is the version proposed for the next shots of the film.</dd>
  <dt>On this site</dt><dd>The side-by-side and reveal videos are frames 734–959 of the nitrate scan and the sixth version, at the 640 × 512 working size. The stills are frames 748 and 826.</dd>
  <dt>Reproduction</dt><dd>Not offered. The material belongs to the George Eastman Museum.</dd>
</dl>
